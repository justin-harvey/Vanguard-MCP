The problem

The smaller the government, the less assurance it can afford.

A Big Four audit is deep, skilled, and necessary work. It is also periodic, sampled, retrospective, and expensive. A firm tests a slice of the transactions, forms a judgment, and issues an opinion you are asked to trust; the evidence behind it stays in the firm's workpapers. That model is priced for large entities. The result is that a small town, a school district, or a county, exactly where public trust is thinnest and every dollar is visible to a resident, gets the least continuous assurance of anyone.

Meanwhile a resident or a council member asking a simple question, "how much of the paving grant was actually spent, and on what?", is handed a figure from a dashboard they have to take on faith. There is no cheap way to trace it to the record and reproduce it later. That gap is the one Vanguard MCP exists to close.

The shift

The evidence, not just the opinion.

Vanguard does not replace your auditor. It produces the evidence packet an audit is built from, and makes it something a resident, a council member, and the auditor can all recompute for themselves. The engagement gets cheaper and faster because the evidence is already assembled, reproducible, and covers every figure instead of a sample.

Where it fits

How this differs from the tools you already know.

None of the parts are new. Guarded text-to-SQL, verification of AI-generated figures, and tamper-evident ledgers all exist on their own. What is distinctive is how tightly Vanguard binds them, so a resident or a council member cannot receive an unverified figure, and every answer leaves a reproducible, tamper-evident record.

For the public sector specifically

The same reconciliation a town already needs.

The core risk is the distance between what an entity reports and what its records actually support. For a municipality that is grant drawdowns, bond-proceeds spending, and year-end reporting: the reported figure set against what the ledger supports, grounded and hash-chained so the gap is a reproducible fact, not an assertion.

Traceable to the record

A resident, a council member, or an auditor asks a plain-English question and gets a number they can trace to the source row and reproduce later, not a figure from a dashboard they take on faith.

Schema-agnostic

Point the engine at a municipal chart of accounts, a grant ledger, or a checkbook register. The guard, grounding, lineage, and audit chain are identical; a new dataset is a new allow-list, not new plumbing.

In keeping with our own discipline

What this is, and what it is not.

It produces evidence, not a certification. A SOC 2 report or an audit opinion is something a CPA firm issues about an organisation. Vanguard produces the evidence that rests under it: a query, its provenance, a reproducible hash of its result, and a chain showing the record has not been edited.

It does not replace professional judgment. It makes the judgment cheaper to exercise and the result verifiable by anyone, including the people whose money it is.

It refuses more than a chatbot, by design. An unmatched figure is a refusal, not a warning. For public money, that trade-off is the requirement.

This demo runs on synthetic values. The reporting-gap study's aggregates reconcile to real, cited filings; its transaction-level rows are synthetic and labelled. For a tool about traceable figures, fabricated data presented as real is the exact failure it exists to prevent.
